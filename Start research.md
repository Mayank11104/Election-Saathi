India’s Electoral Infrastructure and Civic Administration: A Comprehensive Knowledge BaseThe democratic framework of the Republic of India represents the most massive logistical, legal, and civic exercise in human history. Administered by the Election Commission of India (ECI) under the absolute constitutional mandate of Article 324, the electoral process is governed primarily by the Representation of the People Act (RPA), 1950, and the Representation of the People Act, 1951, alongside the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961\. The following comprehensive research report details the exhaustive statutory, procedural, and technical architecture of the Indian General Elections. This document is explicitly engineered to serve as the foundational knowledge base for advanced civic technology applications, providing verified, production-grade legal and technical intelligence.1. The Eight Phases of the Indian General ElectionThe Indian electoral cycle is a continuous administrative mechanism, yet the active election period is statutorily defined through a strict sequence of formal phases. The Election Commission of India, acting as an autonomous constitutional authority, executes these phases with absolute statutory authority, ensuring that the transition of power remains seamless and transparent.Phase 1: Pre-Election Planning and Electoral Roll RevisionBefore any formal announcement, the foundation of the election is laid through the Special Summary Revision (SSR) of electoral rolls. The Election Commission of India, operating through state Chief Electoral Officers (CEOs) and district Electoral Registration Officers (EROs), undertakes this phase to ensure absolute demographic fidelity. The legal basis for this phase resides in Sections 14 and 15 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950, which establishes the qualifying dates for voter eligibility. During this continuous phase, citizens must proactively verify their names on the electoral roll and submit necessary registration or correction forms. The critical deadline for citizens is the freezing of the electoral rolls, which conventionally occurs just prior to the formal nomination period.Phase 2: Election Announcement and NotificationThe active electoral machinery is triggered by the ECI's formal press conference announcing the election schedule. The exact timeline varies based on the expiration of the preceding Lok Sabha's term. At the precise moment of this announcement, the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) immediately comes into force. Following the ECI's announcement, the President of India (for the Lok Sabha) issues a formal Gazette Notification under Section 30 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951\. This legal instrument officially calls upon the parliamentary constituencies to elect their members. Citizens must be aware that from this moment, the ruling government is legally barred from announcing new financial grants or welfare schemes.Phase 3: Nomination of CandidatesCommencing on the date of the Gazette Notification, the nomination phase allows candidates to formally declare their intent to contest. The Returning Officer (RO) of each constituency acts as the statutory authority responsible for receiving these nominations. The exact duration typically spans seven to eight days. The legal basis is governed by Sections 31 and 33 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951\. For citizens, this phase is critical for transparency, as candidates must file Form 26 (an affidavit detailing their criminal antecedents, financial assets, and educational qualifications), fulfilling the Supreme Court's mandate established in Association for Democratic Reforms (2002). The absolute deadline for candidates to submit their papers is strictly enforced at 3:00 PM on the final designated day.Phase 4: Scrutiny of NominationsImmediately following the close of nominations, the Returning Officer conducts a quasi-judicial hearing to scrutinize all submitted nomination papers. Operating under Section 36 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the RO meticulously examines the validity of proposers, security deposits, and the completeness of the Form 26 affidavits. Invalid nominations are summarily rejected. Citizens hold a passive role during this phase, but candidates and their election agents must be present to defend their submissions against potential legal objections from rival factions.Phase 5: Withdrawal of CandidatureTo allow for political consolidation and the elimination of non-serious contenders, a statutory withdrawal window is provided. Governed by Section 37 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, candidates are typically granted two days post-scrutiny to formally withdraw their nominations. Once the deadline expires (again, strictly at 3:00 PM), the Returning Officer publishes the final, immutable list of contesting candidates and officially allots election symbols. Citizens must utilize this final list to inform their voting decisions.Phase 6: The Electoral CampaignThe campaign phase represents the core democratic engagement period, running from the publication of the final candidate list until the statutory silence period begins. The ECI, DEOs, and Expenditure Observers rigorously monitor this phase to ensure absolute compliance with the Model Code of Conduct. Candidates engage in public rallies, media advertising, and door-to-door canvassing. Citizens must remain vigilant during this phase, utilizing tools like the cVIGIL app to report instances of voter bribery, illicit liquor distribution, or hate speech. The legal boundaries of campaigning are defined by the Representation of the People Act, 1951, and overlapping sections of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita addressing corrupt electoral practices.Phase 7: The Silence Period and Polling DayTo afford voters an uninfluenced period of reflection, Section 126 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, mandates a strict 48-hour "Silence Period" leading up to the conclusion of the poll. During this window, all public meetings, loudspeaker usage, and active campaigning are explicitly prohibited. Polling day itself is governed by the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961\. The Presiding Officer assumes absolute authority over the polling station. Citizens must arrive at the polling booth with an approved identity document before the official closing hour. Any citizen standing in the queue at the exact closing time possesses a statutory right to cast their vote, regardless of how long it takes to clear the line.Phase 8: Counting of Votes and Declaration of ResultsThe culmination of the electoral cycle occurs on a synchronized counting day. The Returning Officer supervises the unsealing of the Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) and the tabulation of votes under Section 64 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951\. A critical component of this phase is the mandatory audit of Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) slips from five randomly selected polling stations per Assembly Segment, as mandated by the Supreme Court of India. Upon the conclusion of the tally, the Returning Officer legally declares the winner under Section 66 of the RPA 1951 and issues the formal Certificate of Election.2. Polling Day Operations and Civic ParticipationThe operational execution of polling day is meticulously detailed in the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961, designed to balance throughput efficiency with absolute ballot secrecy. The ECI has decoupled the physical Elector's Photo Identity Card (EPIC) from the strict right to vote. While possessing an EPIC is standard, a citizen whose name is explicitly present on the finalized electoral roll can cast their vote using alternative identification.The 12 Approved Identity DocumentsIf a voter does not possess an EPIC, the ECI officially mandates the acceptance of 12 alternative photo identity documents at the polling station. These include:Aadhaar CardMNREGA Job CardPassbooks with photograph issued by a Bank or Post OfficeHealth Insurance Smart Card issued under the Ministry of Labour / Ayushman BharatDriving LicensePAN CardSmart Card issued by the RGI under the National Population Register (NPR)Indian PassportPension document with a photographService Identity Cards with a photograph issued by Central/State Govt./PSUs/Public Limited CompaniesOfficial identity cards issued to MPs, MLAs, and MLCsUnique Disability ID (UDID) Card issued by the Ministry of Social Justice & EmpowermentIt is crucial to note that the Voter Information Slip (VIS) distributed by Booth Level Officers prior to the election is strictly for guidance (to locate the serial number and polling booth) and cannot be used as an identity proof.The Polling Booth WorkflowThe internal architecture of an Indian polling station operates as an optimized assembly line, supervised by the Presiding Officer (PrO) and executed by a team of Polling Officers (PO).Identification (PO-1): Upon entering, the voter approaches the First Polling Officer, who is in charge of the marked copy of the electoral roll. PO-1 verifies the voter's ID document and loudly calls out the voter's serial number and name. This auditory declaration allows the candidate's polling agents, seated nearby, to challenge the voter's identity if they suspect impersonation.Verification and Registration (PO-2): The voter proceeds to the Second Polling Officer. PO-2 applies a specialized indelible ink to the voter's left forefinger. The officer then records the voter's serial number and the specific ID document presented in the statutory Register of Voters (Form 17A) and obtains the voter's signature or thumb impression. Finally, PO-2 issues a serialized voter slip to the citizen.EVM Activation (PO-3): The voter hands the serialized slip to the Third Polling Officer. PO-3 verifies the presence of the indelible ink on the finger and presses the "Ballot" button on the EVM Control Unit. This action electronically enables the Ballot Unit located inside the private voting compartment.The Voting Action: The voter proceeds alone into the voting compartment, shielded by privacy boards. They press the blue button adjacent to the name and symbol of their chosen candidate on the Ballot Unit. A red LED illuminates beside the candidate's name. Simultaneously, the Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) printer generates a paper slip containing the candidate's serial number, name, and party symbol. This slip remains visible behind a transparent window for exactly seven seconds. Subsequently, the VVPAT cuts the slip, dropping it into a sealed internal drop-box, and the Control Unit emits a loud, continuous beep confirming the successful registration of the vote.Critical Protocols and Booth Edge CasesThe Presiding Officer manages several edge cases that standard citizens may encounter.Missing Names: If a citizen arrives with a valid EPIC but their name is completely missing from the printed electoral roll, they are legally barred from voting. No on-the-spot registration or manual inclusion is permitted on polling day.Name Mismatches: Minor clerical errors or spelling discrepancies between the voter's ID document and the electoral roll are legally overlooked. If the Presiding Officer is satisfied that the individual standing before them is the genuine elector intended by the roll, the vote is permitted.Impersonation and Tendered Votes: If a voter arrives at the booth only to be informed that someone else has already cast a vote against their name, they are not disenfranchised. Under Rule 42 of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961, the Presiding Officer will subject the voter to rigorous identity verification. Once satisfied, the officer issues a physical "Tendered Ballot Paper." The voter marks their choice manually, places it in an envelope, and hands it to the Presiding Officer. This vote is not recorded on the EVM but is securely preserved for judicial scrutiny if the election margin is narrower than the number of tendered votes.Booth Agent Rights: Recognized political candidates appoint Polling Agents to sit inside the booth. They possess the statutory right to view the face of every entering voter, challenge suspicious identities by depositing a prescribed fee, and observe the Presiding Officer sealing the EVM and VVPAT hardware at the conclusion of the poll.Prohibitions on Media and Electronics: The sanctity of the secret ballot is absolute. The use of mobile phones, cameras, spy pens, or any recording devices inside the polling booth is strictly prohibited. Photographing one's vote on the EVM is a criminal offense.PwD Voting Rights and the SVEEP ArchitectureThe Systematic Voters' Education and Electoral Participation (SVEEP) program is the Election Commission's premier multi-intervention initiative designed to promote voter literacy and electoral engagement. Operating under the guiding ethos of "No Voter to be Left Behind," SVEEP specifically targets youth apathy, gender gaps, and the disenfranchisement of marginalized communities.A core pillar of SVEEP is the enfranchisement of Persons with Disabilities (PwD). The ECI mandates Assured Minimum Facilities (AMF) at every polling station, guaranteeing wheelchair ramps and accessible infrastructure. PwD voters, including the visually impaired, are granted priority access, bypassing standard queues. The EVM Ballot Units feature Braille signage corresponding to candidate serial numbers. Furthermore, under the Conduct of Election Rules, visually impaired or infirm voters possess the right to bring a companion of their choice into the voting compartment to assist them in casting their vote, a detail formally recorded by the Presiding Officer in Form 17A.4. EVM and VVPAT: Deep Technical Security and Auditing MechanicsThe Indian Electronic Voting Machine (EVM) ecosystem represents a unique paradigm in electoral technology. Unlike commercial hardware, Indian EVMs are proprietary, closed-system devices designed and manufactured exclusively by two highly guarded central public sector undertakings: Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL) and Electronics Corporation of India Limited (ECIL).Technical Security FeaturesThe assertion that Indian EVMs cannot be hacked remotely rests on verifiable hardware realities rather than mere software encryption.Absolute Standalone Architecture: The EVMs (comprising the Control Unit, Ballot Unit, and VVPAT) are strictly standalone machines. They are entirely disconnected from the internet, intranet, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, or any internal/external radio-frequency networks. The hardware fundamentally lacks any wireless receivers, decoders, or modems.One-Time Programmable (OTP) Microcontrollers: The core software code is written in-house by BEL and ECIL engineers. This machine code is burnt into the microcontrollers during the manufacturing phase. Because the chips are inherently One-Time Programmable, the software can never be read, altered, patched, or overwritten after it is fused.Dynamic Key Coding: The modern M3 model EVMs employ advanced dynamic encryption for transmitting key-press data from the Ballot Unit to the Control Unit. Every time a voter presses a button, the signal is encrypted dynamically, neutralizing any attempt to intercept the cable or insert malicious hardware to sequence votes.Unauthorized Access Detection Module (UADM): The M3 EVMs are equipped with an advanced tamper-detection perimeter. Any physical attempt to pry open the machine's casing or access the internal circuitry instantly triggers the UADM, rendering the entire machine permanently inoperative (factory reset required).The First Level Checking (FLC) ProcessThe security protocol begins months before the actual election. The First Level Checking (FLC) is an exhaustive physical and technical diagnostic operation conducted at district headquarters. This process is executed exclusively by authorized BEL/ECIL engineers under the continuous supervision of the District Election Officer (DEO). Crucially, the FLC is conducted in the mandatory presence of representatives from recognized national and state political parties. During FLC, engineers clear any residual memory, check for physical tampering, and conduct a massive mock poll of at least 1,000 votes on a 5% random sample of the EVMs to mathematically prove tally accuracy.Mock Poll Execution on Election DayThe integrity of the EVM is demonstrated to the political stakeholders on the morning of the election. Exactly 90 minutes before the official commencement of public polling, the Presiding Officer conducts a mandatory Mock Poll at every single polling station. Polling agents appointed by the candidates randomly cast a minimum of 50 votes. The Presiding Officer then closes the poll on the Control Unit, tallies the electronic result against the physical slips printed by the VVPAT, and proves the machine's accuracy. Most importantly, the Presiding Officer must execute the CRC protocol—Close the mock poll, view the Result, and Clear the memory—before officially sealing the Control Unit with cryptographic Pink Paper Seals signed by the polling agents.The VVPAT Audit ProcessThe Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail (VVPAT) bridges the gap between digital efficiency and physical tangibility. Following the 2013 Supreme Court directive in PUCL v. Union of India, VVPATs were integrated into the EVM ecosystem. To provide a definitive statistical guarantee of electoral fidelity, the Supreme Court mandated a rigorous post-election audit. On counting day, the physical VVPAT slips of exactly five randomly selected polling stations per Assembly Segment (or within a Parliamentary Constituency segment) are painstakingly hand-counted by election officials in the presence of candidate counting agents. These physical counts are tallied against the electronic aggregate displayed by the respective Control Units, ensuring absolute correlation.Post-Result Storage and Hardware HistoryFollowing the declaration of results, EVMs are transported under armed Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF) escort to highly secure strong rooms. The machines are locked and sealed for a mandatory statutory period of 45 days. This window exists exclusively to allow aggrieved candidates to file Election Petitions in the relevant High Court. If no petition is filed, the EVM memory is cleared. However, the physical VVPAT slips extracted during the counting process are preserved in sealed black envelopes for exactly one year under Rule 94 of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961.The history of the EVM dates back to 1977, with prototypes developed by ECIL in 1979\. They were first deployed on a pilot basis in 1982 during the by-election for the Parur Assembly Constituency in Kerala. Widespread deployment was delayed until the Representation of the People Act, 1951, was amended in 1988 (inserting Section 61A) to legally authorize electronic voting.5. The Model Code of Conduct (MCC): Coverage and EnforcementThe Model Code of Conduct (MCC) is a comprehensive set of ethical and operational guidelines issued by the Election Commission of India. It is designed to regulate the behavior of political parties, contesting candidates, and the incumbent government during the election period. Although it lacks direct, standalone statutory backing, it possesses immense coercive power. The ECI enforces the MCC through its constitutional authority under Article 324, and many MCC provisions directly overlap with criminal statutes in the Indian Penal Code (now Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita) and the Representation of the People Act, 1951.Operational Timeline: The MCC comes into force the exact moment the ECI formally announces the election schedule in its press conference. It remains in continuous operation until the official notification of the final election results is published.Prohibited ActivitiesTo prevent the ruling party from leveraging the state apparatus to gain an unfair electoral advantage, the MCC strictly prohibits :Government Functionaries: The announcement of new financial grants, the laying of foundation stones for new public projects, or the initiation of new welfare schemes.State Resources: The monopolization of public spaces, state-owned rest houses, or helipads. Ministers are explicitly banned from using official government vehicles, aircraft, or state personnel for electioneering or campaign transit. Furthermore, using the public exchequer to issue advertisements highlighting government achievements is strictly illegal.Campaign Behavior: Candidates cannot appeal to voters based on caste, religion, or communal sentiments. Generating mutual hatred or tension between linguistic or religious communities is forbidden. Using places of worship (mosques, temples, churches) as forums for political propaganda is completely banned.Corrupt Practices: Bribing, intimidating, or impersonating voters is prohibited, as is the distribution of liquor or freebies to influence the electorate.Allowed ActivitiesThe MCC does not halt the machinery of the state; it merely curtails partisan exploitation. Allowed activities include :The continuation and execution of previously sanctioned, ongoing welfare schemes (provided no new beneficiaries are added for political gain).Criticism of opposing political parties, provided it remains strictly confined to their policies, programmatic failures, and past governance records. Personal attacks regarding the private lives of opposition leaders are explicitly forbidden.Enforcement via cVIGIL and PenaltiesThe ECI revolutionized the enforcement of the MCC by introducing the cVIGIL mobile application. This platform empowers ordinary citizens to act as election observers. If a citizen witnesses an MCC violation (such as cash distribution or illegal hoarding), they can capture a live, geo-tagged, and time-stamped photograph or video. The app deliberately blocks uploads from the phone's gallery to ensure the evidence is live and unmanipulated.Submitting a complaint triggers a strict 100-minute Service Level Agreement (SLA). The District Control Room instantly assigns the GPS coordinates to the nearest Flying Squad Team (FST), which must navigate to the location, intercept the violation, and report to the Returning Officer. The RO takes decisive action and updates the citizen within the 100-minute window.Penalties for MCC violations range from the ECI issuing formal censures and reprimands, to imposing temporary campaign bans (gag orders) on star campaigners, to filing formal First Information Reports (FIRs) for bribery or hate speech under the RPA 1951\. Historic implementations of the MCC have seen the ECI repeatedly gag Chief Ministers and prominent party leaders for communal speeches, demonstrating the absolute, non-partisan authority of the Commission.6. Constitutional Voter Rights and Judicial EntitlementsThe architecture of voter rights in India is a complex interplay between the Constitution, parliamentary statutes, and landmark Supreme Court judgments.The Right to Vote: The fundamental bedrock of Indian democracy is Article 326 of the Constitution, which establishes the principle of Universal Adult Suffrage. However, the Supreme Court has consistently held that the tangible act of voting is not a fundamental constitutional right, but a statutory right governed by Section 62 of the Representation of the People Act, 1951\. Therefore, it is subject to statutory limitations and disqualifications, such as individuals confined in prison being barred from voting.The Right to a Secret Ballot: The secrecy of the voter's choice is a non-negotiable imperative protected by Section 128 of the RPA 1951 and Rules 39 and 49-M of the Conduct of Elections Rules, 1961\. Disclosing one's vote or attempting to photograph the EVM interface compromises this secrecy and invites immediate penal action.Right to a Paid Holiday: To ensure that economic vulnerabilities do not result in disenfranchisement, Section 135B of the RPA 1951 guarantees a paid holiday on polling day. Every registered elector employed in any business, trade, industrial undertaking, or commercial establishment (including daily wage and casual workers) is entitled to this holiday. Employers who deduct wages or force attendance on polling day face statutory fines and legal action.Right to Information (Candidate Affidavits): Following the landmark Supreme Court judgment in Union of India v. Association for Democratic Reforms (2002), it was established that a voter's right to know the background of contesting candidates flows directly from the fundamental right to freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a). Candidates are now legally bound to file Form 26 affidavits, and the ECI provides this data directly to voters via the "KYC (Know Your Candidate)" mobile application.The Right to NOTA: In 2013, the Supreme Court delivered a historic judgment in People's Union for Civil Liberties (PUCL) v. Union of India, recognizing the voter's right to register a negative vote while maintaining absolute ballot secrecy. The ECI subsequently integrated the "None of the Above" (NOTA) button as the final option on all EVM Ballot Units.NOTA Mechanics: While NOTA empowers voters to express systemic dissatisfaction, it possesses absolutely no electoral veto power. Even if NOTA secures the highest number of votes in a constituency, it does not invalidate the election. The human candidate securing the second-highest number of valid votes is legally declared the winner.Grievance Redressal: If a voter faces intimidation, finds their booth inaccessible, or is wrongfully denied their right to vote, the ECI provides a centralized redressal matrix through the National Voter Helpline (1950). Observers stationed in the district also hold direct authority to intervene in cases of voter suppression.7. The Mechanics of Government FormationThe translation of electoral mandates into executive governance in India follows the Westminster model of parliamentary democracy, heavily reliant on constitutional conventions.The Majority Calculation: The Lok Sabha is composed of 543 elected Members of Parliament. Government formation requires a political party or a pre-poll coalition to command the absolute majority of the House.The Magic Number: The threshold required to form the Union Government is exactly half the total seats plus one. Therefore, 272 seats constitute the "magic number" required for an absolute majority.Hung Parliament Protocol: A "Hung Parliament" occurs when the electorate delivers a fractured mandate, and no single party or pre-poll alliance manages to secure the requisite 272 seats. In this scenario, the constitutional discretion of the President of India is activated. Convention dictates that the President will invite the leader of the single largest party, or the leader of the largest post-poll coalition, to explore the possibility of forming a government.The Floor Test / Vote of Confidence: The leader invited by the President is sworn in as Prime Minister but is given a strict timeline (conventionally ranging from a few days to two weeks) to prove their majority on the floor of the Lok Sabha. This "Vote of Confidence" or "Floor Test" requires the Prime Minister to secure a simple majority (more 'Ayes' than 'Nays' from the members present and voting). If the motion fails, the government collapses immediately.Coalition Dynamics: If no single party holds 272 seats, multiple parties may pool their elected members to cross the threshold, forming a coalition government. These coalitions often draft a "Common Minimum Program" to align their disparate political ideologies for governance.The Role of the Speaker: Once the newly elected Lok Sabha convenes, the MPs elect a Speaker from among themselves. The Speaker assumes a vital constitutional role as the impartial presiding officer of the House. They manage parliamentary debate, act as the sole adjudicator for disqualifications under the Anti-Defection Law (Tenth Schedule), and exercise a casting vote in the event of a tie during a floor test.Failure to Form a Government: If the floor test fails and no alternative coalition can demonstrate a viable majority to the President's satisfaction, the President dissolves the Lok Sabha, plunging the nation into fresh, snap elections.8. Key Electoral Officials: Hierarchical Roles and PowersThe execution of the Indian election requires the deputation of millions of state employees to the ECI, operating through a strict hierarchical command structure.Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) & Election Commissioners (EC): The apex constitutional authority. Comprising one CEC and two ECs, this body wields absolute power under Article 324 for the superintendence, direction, and control of all national and state elections. They possess equal voting power in policy decisions.State Chief Electoral Officer (CEO): The highest-ranking ECI representative within a specific state. The CEO supervises the entire state's electoral logistics, CAPF deployment, and inter-departmental coordination, ensuring ECI directives are executed at the macro level.District Election Officer (DEO): Typically the District Magistrate (DM) or Collector. The DEO is the focal point for district-level election management. They manage the FLC of EVMs, coordinate local police deployments, secure the EVM strong rooms, and maintain the absolute law and order of the district during the election cycle.Returning Officer (RO): The supreme statutory authority for a specific Parliamentary or Assembly Constituency. The RO accepts candidate nominations, scrutinizes affidavits, allocates party symbols, supervises the counting of votes, and legally issues the winning certificate.Electoral Registration Officer (ERO): A statutory officer (usually a Sub-Divisional Magistrate) dedicated exclusively to the preparation, continuous revision, and purification of the electoral roll. The ERO is the final adjudicating authority for Form 6, 7, and 8 applications.Booth Level Officer (BLO): The grass-root, ground-level representative of the ECI, often a local teacher or clerk. BLOs conduct physical door-to-door field verification for voter registration forms and are responsible for distributing Voter Information Slips (VIS) to households prior to polling day.Presiding Officer (PrO): The absolute authority inside a specific polling station on election day. The PrO conducts the morning mock poll, cryptographically seals the EVM and VVPAT hardware, ensures ballot secrecy, adjudicates tendered/challenged votes, and enforces the MCC within the 100-meter perimeter of the booth.Polling Officer (PO): The booth assistants working under the PrO. PO-1 verifies voter identities against the roll. PO-2 applies indelible ink, logs the voter in Form 17A, and issues the voter slip. PO-3 operates the EVM Control Unit.Observers (General, Expenditure, Police): Senior IAS, IPS, or IRS officers deputed directly by the ECI from outside the state. They act as the "eyes and ears" of the Commission, immune to local political pressure. They monitor logistical fairness, track illegal candidate spending, and ensure police neutrality.Micro Observer: Central government employees deployed randomly to highly sensitive or critical polling stations. They sit inside the booth, strictly observing the Presiding Officer's conduct, ensuring the mock poll is executed correctly, and reporting directly to the General Observer.10.
